How payouts work

A coin launched with a fee split holds its fees in the coin's own vault until someone pays them out. Pay out sends each shareholder their share, so the platform share and yours move together.

Payouts land as SOL in the wallet that launched the coin. To move it, use Sweep on the Launch page, which links the destination to that wallet on-chain.

Docs

BurnerPad is a launcher, not a mixer. It makes a fresh wallet in your browser, so your main wallet stays out of the launch transaction. It does not hide where the money came from.

The flow

  1. 1Make a burnerYour browser creates a new wallet. The key never leaves your device.
  2. 2Fund itSend it SOL from a wallet or exchange you choose.
  3. 3Pick a venuepump.fun, Meteora or Raydium LaunchLab. Add a name, ticker and image.
  4. 4LaunchThe burner signs the launch and your first buy, in one transaction where the venue allows.
  5. 5Listed and checkedBurnerPad verifies it on-chain and shows public checks on the coin page.
  6. 6Earn feesTraders pay trading fees. A share is yours, set by the venue.
  7. 7ClaimClaim fees on the Claim fees page, with the burner's key.
  8. 8SweepMove what is left out of the burner, when you choose to.

Burner wallets

A burner is a new keypair made in this browser. BurnerPad never sees the private key and never stores it. You fund it yourself, so BurnerPad never holds your money.

  • Back it up before you fund it. Use Download backup on the launch page. Clearing browser data can erase the key, and a lost key means lost funds.
  • Phantom works too. You can launch from a wallet you already have. Then the launch is linked to that wallet.
  • Sweep is optional. It sends leftover SOL to an address you choose. Doing that links the two wallets on-chain.

Venues

pump.fun

Bonding curve that graduates to PumpSwap. Creator fees can be shared at launch with a fee-split transaction you sign.

Meteora

Dynamic bonding curve that graduates to a DAMM pool with its liquidity locked. Fees are part of the curve config, so there is no split to sign.

Raydium LaunchLab

1B supply, 793.1M sold on the curve, 85 SOL raised before it graduates to a Raydium pool. Fees are set in the platform account.

Claiming

  • Where: the Claim fees page. Load the burner saved in this browser, paste its key, or connect a wallet.
  • pump.fun: pay out a coin to send each shareholder their share. Anyone can trigger a payout.
  • Meteora: claim per coin, from the creator wallet that launched it.
  • Raydium LaunchLab: one balance per creator wallet, across all its coins. Claims arrive as plain SOL.

Coin checks

Every coin page shows public checks read straight from the chain: whether the mint and freeze authorities are revoked, how much the creator and the top ten holders own, who bought in the launch block, and what the platform fee is.

What it does not do

  • No pool, no custody. There is no deposit-and-withdraw pool. Nobody but you can move your funds.
  • Funding is visible. If you fund the burner straight from your main wallet, anyone can see the link on a block explorer. For real separation, fund it from an account that is not tied to you.
  • Sweeping links wallets. Sending leftovers or fees to your main wallet links the two. Keep them in the burner if separation matters.
  • Lose the key, lose the money. Back it up first.

FAQ

Can BurnerPad move my funds?

No. Every transaction is signed in your browser. The server only stores coin names, images and the launch record.

Is this anonymous?

No. It separates your main wallet from the launch, but the funding transfer is public. Treat it as separation, not secrecy.

What does Devnet mean?

A practice network with free test SOL. Nothing on it has value. The badge at the top shows which network you are on.

Does BurnerPad trade or sell coins?

No. It launches coins and lists them. Trading happens on the venue.

BurnerPad is a non-custodial tool. Tokens are risky; nothing here is financial advice. Check every coin yourself.
